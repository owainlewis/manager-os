# Daily briefing

## When to use
A routine every weekday at 8am, before I start work. Run it by hand if I ask "what did I miss?"

## Why
Most of a manager's day is decided by things that happened while they weren't looking. This briefing reads everything from the last 24 hours so I don't have to, and tells me the few things that need me. The rest can wait.

## Reads
Everything from the last 24 hours (since 8am yesterday, or since Friday 8am on a Monday):
- Email and Slack: messages to me, mentions, and threads in my team's channels
- Linear: tasks created, moved, blocked, finished or past due
- Zoom: transcripts of yesterday's meetings, for actions that name me
- Calendar: today's meetings
- Notion: Me and Goals, and the team page of anyone who comes up

## Steps
1. Read everything above. Note each thing that needs me: a question or request waiting on me, a decision only I can make, a task blocked on me, something that puts a goal at risk, or a person who may need support.
2. Rank them. First: things that block other people, or that someone senior is waiting on. Then: anything that puts a goal at risk. Then the rest.
3. Pick the top three for "Needs you today". For each, say what it is, why it matters today, and the next step I could take. Link the source.
4. List today's meetings in order with what to prepare. For a 1:1, link the person's team page.
5. Summarise what changed in one short list: what shipped, what got blocked, and any decisions made in Slack or meetings.
6. Put everything else under "Can wait", one line each.

## Output
A comment on today's briefing task, or a reply in our conversation if Linear isn't connected. One screen at most.

### Needs you today
1. [What] - [why today] - [next step] ([link])
2. ...
3. ...

### Today's meetings
- [Time] [meeting]: [what to prepare] ([link])

### What changed
- Shipped: ...
- Blocked: ...
- Decided: ...

### Can wait
- ...

## Rules
- Three items at most under "Needs you today". If nothing needs me, say so.
- Skip a section if it's empty.
- Link every item to its source.
- A signal about a person is a reason to ask, never a judgement. Write "Elena hasn't replied on the API review since Monday", not "Elena is disengaged".
- Don't invent context. If something is unclear, say what you'd need to know.
